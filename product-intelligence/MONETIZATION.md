# Monetization Hypotheses

Monetization should follow demonstrated value. Do not use speculative tokenomics as the default business model.

## Candidate ladder

### Free — personal network formation
Purpose:
- lower adoption friction;
- create trusted circles;
- validate real handoff behavior.

Possible limits later:
- active missions;
- route history retention;
- advanced templates.

### Pro — frequent handoff users
Potential paid value:
- repeat route templates;
- trusted-circle management;
- advanced notifications;
- longer audit history;
- branded/shared receipts;
- priority recovery tools.

### Teams
Potential buyers:
- property/event/community/local-business operators;
- equipment-heavy teams;
- organizations with repeated responsibility transfer.

Potential value:
- shared workspace;
- role-based access;
- route templates;
- assignment;
- audit/export;
- analytics;
- team receipts.

### API / integrations
Potential value:
- create handoff mission from another system;
- receive verified lifecycle events;
- embed route receipt;
- connect to ticketing, inventory, property, marketplace, or community systems.

### Per-success pricing
A small fee per successfully verified handoff could be tested only after users already perceive economic value.

## Pricing research questions

- What does a failed handoff cost in time, money, trust, or replacement?
- Is the payer the creator, recipient, team, or marketplace?
- Is pricing clearer per completed route, per seat, or per active workspace?
- Which features are operational necessities versus nice-to-have?
- Does a monetary fee damage willingness to recruit a bridge?

## Do not monetize yet

- user personal data;
- public reputation;
- hidden wallet spread;
- speculative rewards;
- pay-to-rank bridge discovery.
